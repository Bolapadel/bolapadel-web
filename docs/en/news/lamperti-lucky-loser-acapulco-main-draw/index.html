News · Major de México

Lamperti gets in as a lucky loser and will play the Acapulco main draw, his last Premier event

He lost the final qualifying match in two tie-breaks, but a withdrawal has opened a spot. Tonight, with Octavio Álvarez, he faces Goenaga and Collado.

By Luis González · 24 November 2026 · 2 min read

Miguel Lamperti lost last night.

7-6, 7-6 against Aris Patiniotis and Thijs Roper. Two tie-breaks. Almost two hours.

It was the final qualifying match at the Mexico Major. The one that led into the main draw of his last Premier Padel tournament.

It looked like the end.

It wasn't.

The lucky loser spot

A withdrawal from the draw has left a spot open. And that spot goes to Lamperti and Octavio Álvarez, as lucky losers.

His last Premier event will have a main draw.

A year ago, at this very tournament, he got in by coming through qualifying. This time he gets in by another route.

It doesn't matter how. He's in.

Against Goenaga and Collado

It's today, Tuesday, on Cancha Mifel. Second match of the 4pm session in Acapulco. In Spain, around 00:30, in the early hours.

On the other side of the net, Enrique Goenaga and Guillermo Collado.

Goenaga is 24, from Palma, and plays on the right. Collado is 21, from Madrid, and plays on the left.

There are 27 years between Lamperti and Collado.

They've played together since September. At the Kuwait Major they reached the round of 16: along the way they beat Javi Garrido and Juani de Pascual, the kid Lamperti came through qualifying with in Acapulco a year ago. Then they lost to Coello and Tapia.

If Lamperti and Álvarez win, Tino Libaak and Gonzalo Alfonso, the No. 9 seeds and finalists at the Dubai P1, await in the round of 32.

The last dance

Lamperti is 48. He announced in January that 2026 would be his last season at the top level. And after Acapulco, only the Finals in Barcelona remain, reserved for the best pairs of the year. That's why this is his last Premier event.

Yesterday, qualifying closed the door on him in two tie-breaks.

Today, the draw opens it.

The last dance has a main draw. Enjoy it, Miguel.

— Luis González
