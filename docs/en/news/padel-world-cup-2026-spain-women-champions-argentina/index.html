Spain win the women's World Cup in Qatar for a seventh title in a row

Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero beat Osoro and Fassio 6-3, 6-3. Paula Josemaría and Gemma Triay sealed the 2-0 against Brea and Jensen: 6-2, 6-7, 6-3. A tenth World Cup for Spain.

By Luis González · 7 November 2026 · 3 min read

Spain are world champions. Again.

2-0 over Argentina in the final in Doha. A seventh World Cup in a row.

The title-winning point went to Paula Josemaría and Gemma Triay. On the other side of the net was Delfi Brea, Gemma's partner on the tour.

A week ago they won the Kuwait Major together.

The final

  • Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero: 6-3, 6-3 over Aranza Osoro and Martina Fassio, in an hour and 29 minutes.
  • Paula Josemaría and Gemma Triay: 6-2, 6-7, 6-3 over Delfi Brea and Claudia Jensen.

Claudia Fernández and Bea González, down for the third match, didn't get on court.

Ari and Andrea, seven days later

Last Saturday, Ari and Andrea lost the Kuwait final. Against Gemma and Delfi: 3-6, 6-1, 6-1.

Yesterday they didn't set foot on court in the semi-final.

Today they opened the World Cup final. Without dropping a set.

Andrea is 19.

The only set

Paula and Gemma took the first 6-2. The second slipped away in the tie-break.

It was the only set Spain lost in the whole World Cup. Sixteen matches, sixteen wins.

In the third, 6-3.

Gemma and Delfi share the world number one spot. Last Saturday they lifted a trophy together.

Today only one of them could.

It was Gemma.

Ten World Cups

Spain won the women's World Cup in 2014, 2016, 2018, 2021, 2022 and 2024. Now, in 2026. Seven in a row.

Ten in total. Argentina have eight.

It's Carolina Navarro's first as head coach.

Now, the men

The men's final, Spain against Argentina again, follows on the same court. Where to watch, here.

Coello and Tapia, once again, each in their own shirt.

— Luis González
