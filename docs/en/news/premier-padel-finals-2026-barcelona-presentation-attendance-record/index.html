Barcelona presents the 2026 Finals and goes for the attendance record

December 10 to 13, at the Palau Sant Jordi. The organisers have extended the stands to go past 17,000 fans in a single day, something padel has never seen. Tapia, last year's champion, sums it up: "It's finishing the year at home."

By Luis González · 18 November 2026 · 4 min read

Agustín Tapia is Argentine. But when he talks about Barcelona, he says home.

"It's finishing the year at home," he said yesterday at the Saló de Cent of Barcelona City Hall. That's where the Qatar Airways Premier Padel Finals 2026 were presented, in front of more than 150 people.

They are played from December 10 to 13 at the Palau Sant Jordi.

It's the third edition. All three, in Barcelona.

Going for the record

The organisers have extended the stands. The goal: more than 17,000 fans in a single session. It has never happened in padel.

The best figure so far belongs to the Buenos Aires P1: 16,920 people on semi-final Saturday.

Last year, the Barcelona Finals brought together more than 50,000 fans over four days. And more than six million followed them on TV.

Luigi Carraro, president of the FIP, has no doubts: "all the records will be broken."

Joan Cuscó, for the organisers, gave a hint: very few tickets are left for Saturday and Sunday.

Carraro also asked the City Council and the Generalitat to keep backing the event so the Finals stay in Barcelona. Both were in the room: David Escudé, the city's sports councillor, and Berni Álvarez, Catalonia's minister for sport.

Tapia, at home

Tapia lives in the Maresme. He arrived in Spain eight or nine years ago and Barcelona has been his home ever since. So, for him, not having to catch a plane is already a relief. And he's looking forward to playing in front of his family.

The Palau has given him everything.

In 2024, Coki Nieto and Jon Sanz beat him and Coello in the final: 3-6, 7-5 and 6-3. It ended a run of 47 straight wins.

In 2025, Coello and Tapia came back and won it. In the final, against Galán and Chingotto: 6-7, 6-3 and 7-6.

This year they arrive with the number one already secured. They sealed it in Kuwait.

A week ago, they withdrew from the Dubai P1 because of an injury to Tapia. His team has not given a timeline. Yesterday, Tapia was at the presentation.

Ari and Andrea, at home too

Sitting with Tapia on the panel were Ari Sánchez and Andrea Ustero. Ari is from Reus. Andrea is from Barcelona, and she's 19.

Ari already knows what it's like to win here: she was Finals champion in 2024, with Paula Josemaría.

Now she arrives with Andrea. On October 31 they played the Kuwait final together. And eleven days ago they won their match in the World Cup final: 6-3 and 6-3.

Ari spoke of a spectacular year, better than she expected. She likes the challenge of the attendance record. And she shared a detail: on court they talk in Catalan, and their opponents can't understand them.

Andrea wants to end the year the best way possible: in Barcelona, in front of the people who have watched her grow up.

How it works

The 16 best men and the 16 best women of the season qualify. Eight pairs per draw.

  • Thursday 10 and Friday 11: quarter-finals.
  • Saturday 12: semi-finals.
  • Sunday 13: third place and finals.

Before that comes the Mexico Major, from November 23 to 29. It's the last chance to get in.

In 2024, Barcelona took a winning run away from Tapia. In 2025, it gave him a title.

In 2026 he plays at home. And home wants to be fuller than ever.

— Luis González
