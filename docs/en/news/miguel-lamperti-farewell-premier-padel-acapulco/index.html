Lamperti says goodbye to Premier Padel in Acapulco and will keep playing FIP events

With Octavio Álvarez, he lost 7-6, 6-4 in the round of 64 at the Mexico Major, with a tribute included. It isn't a retirement: he's leaving Premier, not his racket.

By Luis González · 25 November 2026 · 3 min read

Miguel Lamperti has played his last match in Premier Padel.

It was last night, in Acapulco. In Spain, it was already the early hours.

With Octavio Álvarez, in the round of 64 at the Mexico Major. On the other side of the net, Enrique Goenaga and Guillermo Collado.

7-6, 6-4. The first set went to a tie-break. The second, to a single break. An hour and a half.

Two tributes in two days

On Monday he lost in qualifying, in two tie-breaks. And there was already a first tribute.

It looked like goodbye. But he got into the draw as a lucky loser and Acapulco gave him one more day.

Last night came the second tribute. The crowd sang that he's Mexican. His fellow players gathered around him on court.

It happened in Acapulco, the city where padel was born.

Don't call it a retirement

He isn't retiring.

Lamperti is leaving Premier Padel. Not his racket.

"If it were up to me, I would never retire, because I love playing," he said.

He'll keep playing some FIP Tour events. He'll do exhibitions and clinics. And he wants to spend a year training to become a coach.

The kids he chose

Lamperti likes playing with youngsters. And he chooses well.

In 2021 he started the season with Arturo Coello. Then came Mike Yanguas. In 2022, Jon Sanz.

On 7 November, all three became world champions with Spain.

In 2025 he played with Dani Santigosa. A year ago, at this very tournament, he came through qualifying with Juani de Pascual. And his last partner in Premier has been Octavio Álvarez. He's 22. Lamperti, 48.

Tonight, in the early hours in Spain, Coello, Yanguas, Sanz, Santigosa and De Pascual play their round of 32 matches in Acapulco.

Lamperti is no longer in the draw.

They are.

Thank you, Miguel

48 years old. More than 25 years of career.

Yesterday we wrote that the last dance had a main draw.

It did. And it had a tribute.

Thank you, Miguel. And keep playing.

— Luis González
