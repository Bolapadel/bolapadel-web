Roglan and Aragón come back against Paquito and Di Nenno and reach the last 16 in Dubai

They came through qualifying and lost the first set 6-0 to the fourth seeds. They won 0-6, 6-4, 6-2. It's their first Premier last 16 together.

By Luis González · 11 November 2026 · 4 min read

Roglan and Aragón lost the first set 6-0.

Across the net: Paquito Navarro and Martín Di Nenno, fourth seeds at the Dubai P1. On centre court.

An hour and a half later, the scoreboard said something else: 0-6, 6-4, 6-2. To them.

"No words, team, what a match!", Aragón said afterwards.

Four matches in four days

Their Dubai started on Sunday, in qualifying.

  • Sunday: 6-2, 6-1 against Moragues and Fonteny.
  • Monday: 6-4, 3-6, 6-4 against Nunes and Sans. They were in the main draw.
  • Tuesday: 6-7, 6-4, 7-6 against Valdés and Núñez, another qualifying pair. Two hours and 43 minutes.
  • Wednesday: Paquito and Di Nenno.

More than seven hours on court in four days. Four wins.

Who they are

Manu Aragón is 20. He's from Campillos (Málaga) and plays on the backhand side.

Albert Roglan is 27. He's from Tortosa and plays on the drive side.

They've been playing together since May. Most of their year has been the FIP tour and Premier qualifying. In September they played the final of the FIP Gold in Bucharest. Ten days ago, the semi-finals in Turin.

In Premier, their ceiling was the round of 32. In June, at the Italy Major, they lost in that round to Lebrón and Augsburger. They had never beaten a seeded pair together in a Premier main draw.

Today, in the same round, they beat the fourth seeds.

What it's worth

Paquito and Di Nenno got back together in June, at that same Italy Major.

Since then, thirteen tournaments. They won at least one match in every one of them. They reached the semi-finals in four.

Dubai was the fourteenth. And today they went out in their first match.

That's not a stat against them. It's the measure of what Roglan and Aragón have done.

On Thursday, a last-16 match against Abbate and Dal Bianco, who also knocked out a seeded pair: 4-6, 6-1, 6-4 against Álex Ruiz and Javi García (13). One of the two pairs will be in the quarter-finals.

The lucky loser pair has a name

Yesterday we reported that a lucky loser pair would take Coello and Tapia's place. They are Marcos González and Diego García.

They lost in qualifying. They got in because the world number ones pulled out. And today they beat Chozas and Rubini: 6-4, 3-6, 7-6, with the third-set tie-break 9-7. Two and a half hours, the longest match of the day.

Marcos is 21 and from Málaga. Diego, 20, from La Línea.

Diego already knows what it's like to win a Premier event. In 2025, aged 18, he won the Gijón P2 with Francisco Cabeza. The final was against Tolito Aguirre and Gonzalo Alfonso.

On Thursday he meets Alfonso again. This time Alfonso plays with Tino Libaak (10). Both played the World Cup final with Argentina on Saturday.

The rest of the day

Lebrón and Augsburger (2) lost the first set to Castaño and Pol Hernández and came back: 4-6, 6-3, 6-3. So did Yanguas and Nieto (5), against Sintes and Zamora: 6-7, 6-3, 6-4. Stupaczuk and Sanz (3) went through when their opponents retired.

In straight sets: Guerrero and Leal (6), Momo and Campagnolo (7), Arce and Tello (8), Goñi and Alonso (9) and Libaak and Alfonso (10).

Women: the top four pairs start on Thursday. Triay and Brea, champions in Kuwait, face Talaván and Saiz.

  • Bautista / Bergamini (11) – Guerrero / Leal (6)

  • Sanyo / Maxi Sánchez (15) – Stupaczuk / Sanz (3)

  • Jofre / Esbrí (12) – Momo / Campagnolo (7)

On Thursday they play in the last 16.
