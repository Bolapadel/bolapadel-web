Why saving Coello for the third point made sense

Before the World Cup final, a lot of people couldn't understand why Juanjo Gutiérrez kept Coello for the last match. I could. I wrote it in a WhatsApp message and never said it here.

By Luis González · 10 November 2026 · 4 min read

A good friend who follows Bola Pádel told me I never gave my opinion on the Coello thing.

He's right.

We covered the final. We covered what Coello, Paquito and Juanjo said in the press room. But I never said what I thought.

And I thought it before it started.

What I wrote before the final

On Saturday, with the pairs already announced, I sent this on WhatsApp:

In English:

Honestly, I don't think it's a bad idea.

In the first one the ball is going to fly, and to counter Leo you've got another big hitter; then it's up to Juan whether he controls the match.

The second one, you write off.

In the third, if you get there, you've got Coello.

I'm copying it as it was. Without tidying it up.

Why it didn't seem crazy to me

On social media, a lot of people saw it the other way round. And I get it.

Saving the world number one for the last match is scary. If you lose the first two, Coello stays on the bench. Watching.

Even he wasn't sure. He said so afterwards in the press room.

I started from one premise: Tapia and Chingotto were going to play the second match together. And without Galán, that match was almost impossible to win.

So you had to go after the first and the third.

And it was no secret. Two weeks before the final, Juanjo had already said it on a VeinteDiez show, with Rodri Ovide, Argentina's head coach, right there: the idea was to put Paquito in the second and go all in on the third.

The Spanish pairs, to me, were clear. The only question was whether Jon Sanz or Mike Yanguas would partner Paquito.

I looked at it match by match.

The first. Across the net was Leo Augsburger, who lives off his smash. For that you had Aimar Goñi, another big hitter. And Juan Lebrón to run the match. It was hard: they lost the first set. But they won it. 5-7, 7-5, 6-2.

The second. Across the net, Tapia and Chingotto. The best pair at the World Cup on paper. "Write it off" sounds harsh, and it's not about Paquito or Jon Sanz. It's about who they had on the other side. Argentina won, 6-1, 6-2.

The third. If you got there, you had Coello. They got there. Coello and Coki Nieto beat Libaak and Alfonso 6-3, 6-2. Spain, world champions.

What about Coello in the first?

Could you put Coello and Coki in the first match? Yes.

But to me it made no sense. This is about winning two matches. And Coello was Juanjo's silver bullet.

And think about the other option. If we were scared of a debutant playing the first match, imagine putting him in the third. All or nothing.

Two years ago, the other way round

In the 2024 final, also in Doha, Coello played the first match. With Coki. They won it 6-1, 6-2.

And Spain lost the World Cup in the third. In the tie-break of the final set.

This time, Juanjo put his best player where finals are decided.

To me, the plan made all the sense in the world.

The credit isn't mine

Getting it right in a WhatsApp message costs nothing. If you're wrong, nobody finds out.

Juanjo took the risk in front of everyone. With a World Cup at stake. And with a lot of people telling him he was wrong.

That's the credit. And it's his.

I just wanted to say what I thought.

Now it's said.

— Luis González
